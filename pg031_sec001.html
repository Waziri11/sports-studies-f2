3. Starting and restarting play

Before the game begins the umpire toss coin where captains of both teams participate. The game begins with a centre pass taken from the centre circle by the Centre (C) player of the team that won the toss. The centre pass alternates between teams after each goal, ensuring fair opportunities for both teams to restart play. Players must stay within their designated starting positions before the whistle is blown.

4. Ball handling

Netball players are not allowed to run with or dribble the ball. Once a pass is received, the player must pass it within three seconds. Additionally, players can pivot on one foot to adjust their position before passing but cannot take extra steps. This rule promotes quick decision-making and continuous ball movement.

5. Scoring

A team scores a goal when either the Goal Shooter (GS) or Goal Attack (GA) successfully shoots the ball through the opponent’s hoop. There are two clear ways to score goals, through open play or by free shots as a result of a technical foul in a goal circle. A goal is only scored from within the shooting circle. If a player shoots from outside the goal circle, the goal does not count. The team with the most goals at the end of the game wins.

6. Contact and obstruction

Physical contact, such as pushing, hitting, or holding an opponent, is not allowed. Any form of contact that interferes with movement of opponent results in a penalty pass to the opposing team. If the contact happens inside the goal circle, the non-offending team may be given a penalty pass or shot.

Defenders must maintain a minimum distance of three feet (0.9 metres) from the player with the ball. If a defender gets too close (within 3 feet or 0.9 metres), they commit an obstruction, giving the attacking team a penalty pass. If the obstruction occurs inside the shooting circle against a shooter, it results in a penalty pass or shot, allowing the shooter a free attempt at the goal.
