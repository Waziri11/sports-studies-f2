Throwing events

Throwing in athletics are field events in which athletes propel an object as far as possible. The sport requires strength, technique, and coordination. The main types of throwing events include javelin throw, discus throw and shot put. In organised throwing events, winners are awarded medals and other ancillary prizes including cash. The known medals are gold medal for the first winner, silver for the second winner and bronze for the third winner.

Precautionary measures in throwing events

Safety measures play a crucial role in throwing events, helping to prevent athletes and spectators from sustaining injuries during performance. Both athletes and officials must prioritise safety by ensuring proper execution of movements and maintaining facilities and equipment. The key safety precautions to be observed before, during and after throwing events include:

  • (a)Perform warm-up exercises before, and cool-down exercises after workouts;
  • (b)Ensure the throwing area is clean by removing any objects that could pose injury risks to athletes and spectators;
  • (c)Ensure that protective barriers, such as throwing cages, are set to control throwing direction and prevent the implement from flying into athletes, officials or spectators that might cause injury;
  • (d)Maintain all equipment and throwing implements in good condition, ensuring they meet the appropriate weight and size requirements based on the athletes’ age and physical maturity;
  • (e)Prepare a fully equipped first aid kit, containing all necessary medical supplies for providing first aid during training and competitions;
  • (f)Ensure that all athletes use proper movement techniques during training and competitions to minimize the risk of injury; and
  • (g)Ensure your body stays hydrated by consuming appropriate fluids, including water to prevent dehydration.
