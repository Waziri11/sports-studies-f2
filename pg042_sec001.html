Rules and regulations for javelin throw

The following are some of the rules for javelin throw:

  • (i)The javelin must meet specific dimensions and weight requirements as per age and sex;
  • (ii)Athletes must hold the javelin by its corded grip and throw it over the shoulder or upper part of the throwing arm;
  • (iii)Throws must be executed within the designated throwing arc at the end of the runway;
  • (iv)The javelin’s metal head must strike the ground first within the marked sector lines;
  • (v)The javelin must land within a landing sector for the throw to be considered valid;
  • (vi)Athletes have 30 seconds to complete their throws after their name is called; and
  • (vii)A throw is considered a foul if the athlete steps over the runway’s boundary lines, the javelin lands outside the sector lines, or if the throw is not completed within the allotted time.

Shot put

Shot put is a field event that involves propelling a heavy, spherical metal ball using a pushing motion.

The throw begins from a position close to the chin or neck and requires a controlled, forceful movement.

The primary goal in the shot put is to achieve the maximum distance possible while staying within the designated throwing area.

The athlete who propels the shot the furthest from the starting point is declared the winner.
