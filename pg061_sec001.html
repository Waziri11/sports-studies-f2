(ii)The jump is measured from the nearest mark made in the sand by any body part from the front edge of the take-off board;
(iii)If the athlete takes off before the take-off board, then the jump shall be measured from the break in the sand to the front edge of the take-off board;
(iv)The athlete must land within the designated landing pit;
(v)A valid attempt must be completed within 30 seconds after being called to jump;
(vi)In standard competitions, each athlete is given three to six attempts, depending on the level of competition;
(vii)If there is a tie, the athlete with the second-best jump is ranked higher; and
(viii)After landing, the jumper must exit the pit from the front or sides to avoid shortening the measured distance.

Triple jump

The triple jump is one of the jumping events whereby the athletes attempt to leap as far as possible from the take-off point after three successive jumps called hop, step and jump. The three jump rhythms are either right-right-left or left-left-right.

Equipment and facilities

The equipment required for the triple jump is the same as those described in the long jump. The triple jump requires a jumping area and a variety of equipment such as tape measures, marking devices, and maintenance tools. Proper facility setup and the right equipment contribute to accurate measurements, athlete’s consistency, and overall event efficiency.
