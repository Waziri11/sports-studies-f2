(g) Beware of water conditions

Be cautious of tides, waves, aquatic animals, and underwater currents when swimming in open waters, as they can be dangerous even for experienced swimmers. Always check weather forecasts and the water temperature before swimming in natural waters.

(h) Avoid swimming alone

Always swim with a companion or in a group to ensure safety in case of an emergency, never swim without the presence of another person.

(i) Stay hydrated and avoid overexertion

Swimming is a physically demanding activity, and dehydration can lead to fatigue. Swimmers should drink enough water before and after swimming and take breaks when feeling exhausted.

(j) Know emergency procedures

Swimmers should be familiar with basic water rescue techniques and CPR (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation) for emergency rescues. Learning how to recognize drowning signs and how to assist a struggling swimmer can help save lives.
