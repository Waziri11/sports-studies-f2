(iv) A steady and complete exhalation makes it easier to take the next breath efficiently.

Timing breathing with arm movements

(i) Each swimming stroke has a specific breathing pattern that must be synchronised with arm movements to maintain momentum;
(ii) In front crawl, swimmers turn their heads to the side during the arm recovery phase to inhale before exhaling underwater;
(iii) In breaststroke and butterfly, swimmers lift their head above water at the right moment to take a quick breath before submerging again; and
(iv) Proper timing ensures that breathing does not interrupt the swimming stroke or slow down movement.

Treading water

Treading water is the ability to stay upright in deep water without sinking. This skill is essential for resting, survival, and maintaining stability in situations where a swimmer needs to stay in one place, such as waiting for rescue or observing the surroundings. Unlike swimming strokes that focus on forward movement, treading water allows the swimmer to remain stationary while keeping the head above water.

To tread water effectively, swimmers must use sculling arm movements to create lift, Figure 3.13. The arms should move in a continuous circular motion, pushing water down and outward to generate buoyancy. This motion helps keep the swimmer afloat without excessive use of energy. The hands should remain relaxed, and small, controlled movements are preferred over forceful splashes. Additionally, swimmers need to use effective kicking techniques to maintain balance.
