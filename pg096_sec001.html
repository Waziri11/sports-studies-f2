Swimming strokes

Swimming strokes refer to the different techniques used to propel the body through water. Each stroke has unique arm movements, leg kicks, and breathing patterns that require coordination for efficient swimming. Mastering these strokes allows swimmers to swim effectively while conserving energy. The four main competitive swimming strokes are front crawl, backstroke, breaststroke, and butterfly.

Front crawl

Front crawl is a swimming stroke which involves flutter kicks with an alternating arm motion in a prone position, where one arm pulls through the water from above the head to the hip while the other recovers above the water. The stroke is considered the fastest and most commonly used stroke in freestyle swimming races. It is favoured for its efficiency, and speed, making it the preferred stroke for long-distance swimming and racing. The following steps will help you practice front crawl:
(i)
Lie in water face-down, keeping the body streamlined to minimize resistance;
(ii)
Move your arms in an alternating windmill motion. One arm extends forward and enters the water, while the other pulls back beneath the body to generate propulsion;
(iii)
Move your legs continuously in a flutter kick to maintain balance, stability, and forward momentum while reducing drag; and
