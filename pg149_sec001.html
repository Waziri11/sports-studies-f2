Holding

In gymnastics, holding refers to the action of maintaining a position on an apparatus for a specific amount of time.

This is often done to demonstrate strength, coordination, stability, agility, and control in a routine.

Holding can be done in various positions, whether on the rings, bars, beam, or floor, and is commonly used to showcase the gymnast’s ability to maintain balance and body control.

The various types of holding including hold on the rings, hold on the bars, arabesque hold, and split hold are primarily classified based on the apparatus and on the hold:

Hold on the rings

Hold on the rings refers to the action of gripping the gymnastic rings tightly with hands and keeping body steady, either by hanging below or holding one-self up above the rings without moving.

This routine includes iron cross and support hold.

(a)

Iron cross: A strength-based skills performed on the rings where the gymnast holds their body suspended with arms extended to the sides, forming a “T” shape.

The following steps will help you practice iron cross:

(i)

Stand under the rings;

(ii)
Hold each ring with one hand, palms facing in. Wrap your fingers tightly around the rings;
(iii)

Jump or push off slightly to lift your feet so you are hanging or holding yourself up;

(iv)

Whether hanging below or holding above, keep your arms straight and locked;

(v)

Keep your legs together and your body firm to avoid swinging;
