Sport Studies for Secondary Schools: Student's Book Form Two

Lifesaving: Skills and techniques used to rescue individuals in water distress.
Marking: Defensive technique where a player closely follows an opponent to restrict movements and passing options.
Measuring tape: A tool used to measure the distance of a jump or throw.
Mental focus: The concentration and psychological preparedness required for executing gymnastics skills safely and effectively.
Mid-court position: The position mainly occupied by the Centre (C) who links defense and attack.
Motor skills: Fundamental learned abilities to move the body and interact with objects using muscles in a specific way to achieve a desired outcome.
Mounts: The techniques a gymnast uses to get onto an apparatus to begin their routine. Mounts vary by apparatus, such as balance beam, uneven bars, and pommel horse.
Non-take-off foot: The opposite foot that helps with balance and leg positioning during the jump.
Obstruction: A foul where a defense stands too close (less than three feet or 0.9 m) to the player with the ball.
Offside: A violation where a player enters an area of the court not permitted to be in according to playing position.
Open water: Natural aquatic environments (lakes, rivers, seas, oceans) used for swimming, training, and competitions.
Overhead pass: A powerful two-handed pass made from above the head, often used to cover longer distances.
Parallel bars: Two bars set at equal heights, used in men’s gymnastics for swinging and strength-based exercises.
Penalty pass: A sanction awarded for major infringements like contact or obstruction.
Pivoting: Rotating on one foot while keeping the landing foot grounded to reposition for a pass or shot.
Breathing control: The technique of managing breath timing and exhalation to maintain swimming rhythm and reduce fatigue.
Recreational pools: Pools designed for leisure swimming, typically in homes or resorts.
Rhythmic gymnastics: A discipline of gymnastics where gymnasts perform routines
